Cinema Studio Namibe: Reviving a Tropical Modernist Icon in Angola
Subscriber Access
|
August 24
Audio available
Exterior of the Cinema Studio Namibe, Angola. Photo credit: Walter Fernandes
What sustains the purpose of architecture that has never been occupied? For decades, this was the question surrounding the Namibe Cinema-Studio, built on the coast of Angola in 1973. Conceived as a film screening venue, the building remained closed and abandoned, never fulfilling the role for which it was created. Yet it withstood the passage of time, becoming an
icon of the Angolan landscape and architecture
, until finally screening its first film nearly fifty years later.
But what kept this building alive in the collective imagination for so long? Was it precisely its bold, singular architecture that was able to give meaning to a project that never had a use?

The 2026
Oslo Architecture Triennale
will take place from September 17 to October 1 at Sofienberg Church in
Oslo
, bringing together more than 30 international architecture projects around the question
"What if Nature Comes First?"
For its ninth edition, the
Triennale
will adopt a two-week festival format, combining an exhibition with conferences, seminars, workshops, film screenings and guided walks. Curated by Line Ramstad, the programme will examine how placing nature at the starting point of architectural decision-making could influence what is built, preserved, transformed or left untouched.

Reused Brick and Timber Shape Reiulf Ramstad Arkitekter's New Railway Station in Sweden
August 24
Audio available
Gothenburg Grand Central station by Reiulf Ramstad Arkitekter. Building site photos, 2026. Image © Kristian Aalerud
Construction is advancing on
Gothenburg
Grand Central, a new station building and prominent western entrance designed by
Reiulf Ramstad Arkitekter
. The project is part of the West Link (Västlänken) commuter rail project at Gothenburg Central Station, consisting of 8 kilometers of railway, including three new stations: Centralen, Haga, and Korsvägen.
The new route is projected to increase capacity
during peak hours and reduce travel times for commuter and regional trains, connecting directly to the existing central station and the Nils Ericson Terminal. The new station is scheduled to open alongside Västlänken's Centralen underground station in December 2026, with office tenants moving in during 2027, and the rest of the tunnel planned to be ready for traffic by December 2030.

The Public Kitchen: How Asian Markets Turn Eating Into Civic Life
Subscriber Access
|
August 23
Audio available
Tekka Centre in Singapore. Image © Richard Horvathd via Wikipedia under license CC BY-SA 3.0
In many Asian cities, eating has never belonged entirely to the private home. It spills into
markets
, hawker centers, street stalls, and wholesale districts embedded within larger buildings or areas. These are places where the city exchanges information and watches other
people pass through
. They are not restaurants in the conventional sense, nor are they simply markets. In some way, their vibrancy and usage reflect the society's conditions at that moment. They are public kitchens: shared infrastructures where
domestic routines
are partially transferred into collective space.
Hong Kong's
Municipal Services Buildings
and curbside
dining
are where food is understood not only as culture, but as urban organization. The cooked-food center in the city folds eating into a larger civic machine of wet markets,
libraries
, and sports halls. The dai pai dong and street-side table, meanwhile, transform the curb into a temporary dining room. Looking beyond Hong Kong, similar questions emerge across the region. How do cities make room for social eating? Where does cooking happen when domestic space is limited? What kind of architecture supports the mess and heat of everyday food?
